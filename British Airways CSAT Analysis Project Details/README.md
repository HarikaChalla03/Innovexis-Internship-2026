# ✈️ British Airways Customer Satisfaction Analysis

##  Project Overview

This project analyzes British Airways customer review data using **Tableau** to understand passenger satisfaction and identify patterns across traveler types, cabin classes, aircraft, geography, and service-quality dimensions.

## Business Objective

The objective was to answer:

* Which passenger segments show different satisfaction levels?
* How does satisfaction vary across cabin classes and aircraft?
* Which service dimensions require further investigation?
* Are there geographic differences in customer experience?
* How can customer feedback be converted into actionable business insights?

##  Tools & Technologies

* Tableau
* Excel / CSV
* Data Cleaning
* Exploratory Data Analysis
* Data Visualization
* Business Analysis

## Key Findings

* Solo travelers recorded **54.47%** satisfaction.
* Family travelers recorded **51.50%** satisfaction.
* Couple travelers recorded **43.25%** satisfaction.
* Business travelers recorded **25.40%** satisfaction.
* Economy passengers had an average overall rating of approximately **4.38** in the analyzed dataset.
* Customer satisfaction varied across aircraft, geography, and service-quality dimensions.

##  Business Insights

1. **Passenger segment matters:** Satisfaction levels differ considerably across traveler types.
2. **CSAT needs deeper analysis:** Overall satisfaction alone does not identify the operational drivers of customer experience.
3. **Service dimensions are actionable:** Staff service, seat comfort, food & beverages, entertainment, ground service, and value for money can help identify improvement areas.
4. **Aircraft-level analysis matters:** Differences between aircraft can help identify areas for further operational investigation.
5. **Geographic segmentation adds context:** Country-level analysis can reveal differences in passenger experience.

##  Business Investigation

The analysis can be used to investigate:

* Why Business travelers report lower satisfaction.
* Which service categories contribute most to dissatisfaction.
* Whether certain aircraft consistently receive lower ratings.
* Whether geographic differences are associated with specific service issues.
* Whether customer dissatisfaction is related to service quality, comfort, or perceived value.

##  Business Recommendations

* Monitor customer satisfaction by **traveler type, aircraft, cabin, country, and service category**.
* Investigate lower-rated service dimensions and identify their operational causes.
* Conduct deeper analysis of aircraft configurations and passenger experience.
* Develop segment-specific customer-experience improvement initiatives.
* Track CSAT trends regularly to measure the effect of operational improvements.

## Bird Species Observation & Conservation Analysis

Tools: Power BI | DAX | Power Query | Excel

# Project Overview

An environmental analytics project analyzing 21K+ bird observations across 127 species to understand species distribution, habitat patterns, observation behavior, environmental conditions, disturbance levels and conservation indicators.

# Business Problem

Conservation teams need a consolidated view of bird observation data to understand where species are being observed, how observation patterns vary across habitats and conditions, and which species may require closer monitoring.

# Business Insights
Insight 1 — Habitat monitoring

Grassland generated substantially more recorded observations than Forest.

Potential use: Conservation teams can investigate whether this difference reflects actual bird activity, habitat characteristics, observation effort, or sampling differences.

Insight 2 — Species concentration

A relatively small number of species account for a noticeable portion of observations, with Vireo olivaceus, Zenaida macroura and Turdus migratorius among the most frequently observed.

Potential use: These species can serve as useful monitoring groups when tracking changes across visits, habitats or seasons.

Insight 3 — Disturbance monitoring

The report separates observations into no, slight, moderate and serious disturbance effects.

Potential use: Conservation teams can identify locations or periods where disturbance should be investigated further.

Insight 4 — Conservation watchlist

The dashboard incorporates PIF Watchlist and Regional Stewardship information and identifies 8 at-risk species.

Potential use: This allows conservation teams to move beyond simple observation counts and focus monitoring on species requiring greater attention.

Insight 5 — Observation methodology matters

The dashboard contains observer, visit, distance and flyover analysis. For example, the report shows different numbers of unique species across visits.

Potential use: Researchers can investigate whether changes in observations are related to actual ecological changes or differences in observation conditions/methodology.

# Business Recommendations
Recommendation 1 — Prioritize habitat monitoring

Use observation volumes by habitat as a starting point for determining where additional monitoring or sampling may be useful.

Recommendation 2 — Monitor at-risk species separately

Create a focused monitoring list for the 8 identified at-risk species, combining observation frequency with watchlist/stewardship status.

Recommendation 3 — Investigate disturbance hotspots

Locations with higher moderate/serious disturbance observations should be investigated further before deciding whether management intervention is required.

Recommendation 4 — Standardize observation methodology

Because the report includes observer, visit and distance dimensions, future studies should maintain consistent observation procedures to make comparisons more reliable.

Recommendation 5 — Track seasonal changes

Use the temporal analysis to establish a recurring monitoring process so conservation teams can identify unusual changes in species activity over time.


# Business Impact

The dashboard provides a centralized analytical view that can help conservation and research teams compare habitats, monitor species activity, identify disturbance patterns and prioritize areas/species for further investigation.
